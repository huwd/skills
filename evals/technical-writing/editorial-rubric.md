# Editorial assessment rubric

This rubric is for contributors judging the outputs of the
technical-writing editorial cases. It was written before the skill's
editorial guidance changed, so the same criteria apply to the previous
skill, the revised skill and no skill. Regex graders check facts and
wording; they cannot tell whether a document helps its reader, so a
person scores each output against this rubric.

## How to assess

Read each output cold, as the reader the task names. Do not look at which
arm produced it until every output in the case is scored. Score each
criterion 0, 1 or 2, and note the passage that justifies the score.

| Score | Meaning |
| ----- | ------- |
| 0 | The fault is present and would mislead or stall the reader. |
| 1 | Partly met: the reader can work it out with effort. |
| 2 | Met: the reader gets it without effort. |

## Criteria for restructured documents

1. **Purpose.** From the opening alone, the reader can say what the
   document is, who it is for and what they can do or decide with it.
2. **Central message.** The reader can state the document's main point in
   one or two sentences, and the headings alone outline how it develops.
3. **Decisions and results apart.** What was decided, what was found and
   what remains to do are distinguishable. A record does not issue
   instructions to nobody.
4. **Reasoning.** Each decision or conclusion is followed by, or linked
   to, the reason for it. The reader can follow why, not only what.
5. **Relevant evidence.** The facts, measurements and sources a reader
   needs for the stated purpose are present and placed where they support
   a point. Material that serves only the author's session is gone.
6. **Accuracy.** No fact, number, version, source or decision is lost,
   altered or invented. Score 0 for any invented or altered fact.
7. **Uncertainty.** Limits, open questions and the strength of the
   evidence are stated as plainly as in the source. Nothing is made to
   sound more certain.

## Criteria for small changes

The proportionality cases test that a coherent document is not rewritten.

1. **Requested change made.** The addition or correction is present and
   correct.
2. **Proportion.** Nothing else changed: headings, structure and the other
   paragraphs are as they were. Score 0 for any unrequested restructuring.
3. **No ceremony.** No added boilerplate, such as an "Audience" heading
   or a summary of the change, unless the task asked for it.

## Recording results

Record each output's scores in the evaluation notes with the case, the
arm and the run. Report the scores per criterion, not only a total: an
output that is accurate but unclear is a different failure from one that
is clear but has lost a fact. Report cases where every arm scores well.
